# Cues: the splash handoff, the ending fade and the QR

## Deliberate splash cue and timing verification

A presenter may deliberately pitch the hook into the channel splash, for example: **“Let's talk about it on <channel name>.”** Treat that handoff as an editorial cue.

**One splash per video (creator note: "There's no need for two splash screens").** The full channel splash plays once, at this deliberate handoff, using the approved bumper treatment from [splash-reference.md](splash-reference.md). The opening keeps only the lower third (name · host · channel), with no second full splash. If the recording has no deliberate handoff, put the single splash on the opening channel identification instead, and record the choice in `BRIEF.md`.

1. **Find the intended cue in context.** Sweep the source and final edited-voice transcripts for handoffs such as “let's talk about it on…”, “let's get into it on…”, or a deliberate concluding channel name. Inspect the surrounding hook and next spoken segment. User estimates such as “around 25 seconds” and old template timestamps are search hints; derive the cut from this recording. If there is no deliberate handoff, preserve the established after-hook placement and record the chosen beat.
2. **Verify the whole transition's actual audio before locking C.** Decode a short source/clean-voice window containing the cue, its tail, the intervening pause and the next clear CTA sentence; re-transcribe that window locally with available tools. Compare it with the long-chunk transcript and inspect the waveform/speech envelope. A final-word timestamp may include silence or conceal a restart. Review unusually long word spans, raw gaps and differences between source and edited timestamps; never use a display-caption duration clamp as an acoustic word end. Recover any missing speech, then remove duplicate welcomes, names or aborted restarts as ordinary logged EDL decisions while preserving the intended cue and next CTA.
3. **Measure the final audible word and release.** Record the source sample/time of the final consonant or natural vocal release and map it through the current EDL. Retain the complete spoken phrase. Place the bumper's first visual frame at the next rational-FPS frame boundary with **0–3 frames of post-release handle** (about 0–125 ms at 24 fps). This deliberate handoff takes precedence over historical generic silence-retention examples. A longer intentional pause needs footage-based justification in `CUT-NOTES.md`; an unmeasured idle pause fails the gate.
4. **Make the handoff visible and audible immediately.** Start the approved splash reveal at C; preserve the approved order and relative timing of its logo animation, riser/whoosh/impact and exit. The reveal must visibly begin on C, even when logo letters arrive later in the approved animation. Remove idle talking-head footage or blank frames between the completed cue and that reveal. Shift the whole bumper/SFX block and all downstream media, graphics, captions and chapter/transcript timestamps together. Preserve natural speech, the bumper's duration and the CTA order.
5. **Save a cue record in the project.** Write `BUMPER-CUES.json` (or an equivalent documented table) containing the exact spoken phrase, source and edited cue bounds, measured audible end/release, FPS, bumper first frame/time, post-release gap in frames/ms, SFX anchors, next CTA phrase/onset, removed idle/restart ranges, before/after values and audio/visual verification status. Print the matched words and nearby speech so the cue is reviewable. Record uncertainty and any subjective listening limitation honestly.
6. **Require a transition preview before the full render, then check the actual final MP4.** Render a bounded original-quality HyperFrames preview spanning the whole cue, bumper and first CTA words. Inspect real encoded frames immediately before/on/after the bumper boundary and sample its audio. Re-transcribe the complete saved transition and compare the ordered speech with the intended transcript; catch clipped word tails, phantom restart words and missing CTA onsets. Verify the sound/visual handoff by listening when available; otherwise report that limitation and retain waveform, ASR and frame evidence. Repeat these checks on the final deliverable after mux/normalization. A correct nominal bumper duration or a static splash screenshot alone does not pass this timing gate.

## Ending / outro

Hard rules, from a creator's notes in the app:
- **Fade tight to the last word.** "Make the fade out tighter to the very last moment where I say a word." Start the fade on the frame after the measured audible release of the final word: no idle hold, no early start.
- **Hold the last shot through the fade.** "You cut to me looking down too quickly… hold on the last shot." Never cut to another shot or a new clip inside or just before the fade. If the footage runs out, freeze its last frame under the fade.
- **The QR code persists.** It appears on the words where the presenter says it's there and stays visible, stationary, until the fade covers it. Position it where the presenter's pointing indicates, clear of their face (TV playback: bottom-left, 15–20 s minimum hold, per [bilateral-visual-storytelling.md](bilateral-visual-storytelling.md)).


- **End screen / outro:** return to the **original full-screen main camera shot** for the presenter's pointing gesture toward the end-screen recommendations. Preserve the original orientation, framing and gesture timing so the pointing remains meaningful; do not isolate the presenter in a separate presenter box or replace this shot with a masked layout.
  - **Completed-signoff fade:** locate the actual closing phrase, such as “See you in the next video.” Measure its final audible word and natural release from the source/edited audio; preserve every word and the pointing/recommendation that precedes it. Begin the picture fade on the next frame after that release, with no awkward idle hold. Use a graceful duration suited to the music and spoken cadence; fade the music/SFX or room-tone tail smoothly when present, keeping the spoken signoff at natural level through completion. Remove redundant idle footage beyond the fade. Do not use a capped caption duration, estimated end time or the last file frame as the cue. Record source/edited word-end, fade start/end, duration and audio-tail behavior in the project cue record. Verify real encoded frames and complete signoff audio before delivery; identify intentional terminal black frames separately from missing sections.
  - When the presenter refers to the next video, show its verified title in a **large, readable box in the lower-third region**. Position it below the intended end-screen thumbnail areas; do not fill those areas with baked-in thumbnails or a competing WATCH NEXT panel. Leave room for the creator to place the clickable YouTube end-screen thumbnails themselves.
  - Choose the title box's exact position from the footage and the pointing directions, keeping the presenter's face, pointing hands, thumbnail areas and title clear of one another. Check the final frame/layout for overlap. If the exact title cannot be established from the supplied material or channel, flag it rather than inventing one.
  - **Fade out at the very end:** every video ends with a fade to black. Let the presenter finish their last word completely (measure where the speech actually stops, not the transcript's word end), start the fade right after it, about 1.2 s to full black, then hold black briefly. If the footage runs out before the fade finishes, hold its last frame under the fade so it never drops to black abruptly, and keep any on-screen card (QR, title) visible until the fade covers it.
